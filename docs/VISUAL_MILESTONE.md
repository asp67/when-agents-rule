# Antiquity visual milestone

This branch introduces the first playable slice of the new direction: warm stone, restrained bronze UI accents, and a battlefield lit as a continuous scene. It is an incremental renderer upgrade, not an engine replacement.

## Open the scene

Serve the repository over HTTP and choose **Explore civilizations** on the local start page. The button opens the Greek scene. Use the civilization picker and **Load civilization** inside the showcase to switch to Egyptian, Yamato or Persian. Loading starts a fresh demo on the same coast; it does not convert an existing settlement in place. Civilization changes replace the current history entry. Back and Main menu remove the showcase routing parameters before resetting, so leaving a demo cannot immediately reopen it. Other URL options and the hosted subpath are preserved.

Direct links use `http://localhost:8080/?showcase=1&civ=greek` (also `egyptian`, `yamato`, `persian`). Omitting `civ` retains the Greek default. The showcase is an explicitly advanced Iron Age campaign start: 11 buildings and 15 units against one rule-based opponent. The lineup includes workers, three infantry tiers, three ranged tiers, three cavalry tiers and a priest. **Inspect workers** brings the camera to the starting workers at close zoom to check faces and headgear.

All four variants use the fixed resource seed `greek-coast-01` and the same settlement layout; the seed name is retained for comparison with earlier previews. No configured model endpoint is used. Advanced starting resources and the settlement are specific to this demonstration, not a fair model comparison. Ordinary campaign and arena starts retain their existing rules.

## What changed

- A shared charcoal, limestone and bronze theme covers the start page, Arena/campaign configuration, model library, live HUD and analyzer. Configuration and library now include nested controls, expanded model settings, authentication hints, model pickers, prompt editors, empty states and action buttons; team colors and connection-status colors remain distinct. The start title uses a system serif font; no fonts or other assets are downloaded.
- Start-menu actions stretch to the longest translated label, with a shared minimum width and a narrow-screen limit. Explore civilizations and the other buttons now match.
- Terrain uses domain-warped meadow/soil coverage, wind-worked sand and layered snow, with continuous dry beach, wet sand, shallows and deep-water transitions. Bright painted bushes, flowers, pebbles and snow dots have been removed. A 1024² colour map supplies broad variation; a separate 256² mipmapped sheet supplies close-up fibre/grain at two world-space scales, fading at distance and underwater. This replaces the former 2048² colour map, reduces texture memory, and adds two texture lookups on the ground without extra draw calls. All surfaces remain procedurally generated at map load.
- Trees, ground cover, stone and gold resources now use separate, matte world materials with restrained bark grain and mineral inclusions. Food bushes use muted fruit clusters. Architecture and unit material painters retain their existing output. Decorative flowers are fewer and quieter; hard-edged flat snow discs are replaced by the terrain blend. Resource positions, amounts and collisions are unchanged.
- Camera controls belong to the minimap frame during play. In the analyzer they occupy the lower-right edge of the viewport. Overview, focus selected, and zoom are visible; the chevron opens reset, rotation and graphics settings. In campaign mode it also contains a pressed-state hand toggle for left-button map dragging. Spectators always use left-drag panning; campaign right-drag pans while right-click retains commands. Touch pans/zooms in either mode, with campaign taps selecting and stationary holds issuing commands on release. Gestures cancel on blur, screen changes or touch cancellation.
- The live transcript mounts eight turns at a time, with **Older**, **Newer** and **Latest** controls for the recorder’s existing 300-turn memory window. Reading older turns holds the page while the arena continues. Decision-log jumps go directly to the requested page; new turns reuse existing cards, and late harness results update their own section. Unchanged polls do no DOM or layout work. The panel uses an opaque surface and layout/paint containment, without backdrop blur over the moving arena. Recording and full-match export retain their existing behavior.
- Directional cast shadows use a WebGL 1 colour/depth framebuffer and a nine-sample tent filter. Its projection is snapped in the light’s image plane. Each filter tap compares against the receiving surface’s plane depth with a small texel-scaled precision margin, instead of applying one centre depth to every sample; this reduces distance-dependent self-shadow speckling. Dithering is disabled while writing packed RG depth and restored for scene drawing. Only opaque geometry already admitted to the visible scene is submitted as a caster. Remembered/translucent entities do not gain solid shadows.
- Lighting now accounts for nonuniform model scaling, adds cool sky fill against warm sunlight, and preserves bright stone with a mild tone curve. Metallic surfaces receive a restrained highlight.
- Since build 1174 Atmospheric lighting adds its light up in linear space (`EngineAtmosphere.lighting`, the square and square root standing in for the display curve). The key light is the sun or the moon; fill comes from the sky above and, warmer and weaker, from the lit ground below. By day the key is solved so that flat ground in full sun has the colour it had before, since every ground palette was painted against that; what changes is everything not in full sun: shade is deeper and takes the sky's colour, walls turn through the light instead of stepping, and dusk is a low warm sun over blue shade. The night is brighter and bluer, with a moon that still casts shadows and a light along the edges of figures, so units can be told from the ground. The roll-off for bright surfaces works on the brightest channel and scales the colour by it, so plaster and lit sand keep their hue. The worn ground under buildings is lit like the ground it lies on (it was drawn at one brightness whatever the hour). Simple lighting is unchanged and remains the comparison. Same single pass; the cost on office machines has not been measured.
- Since build 1175 buildings, trees and rocks sit in soft darkness where they meet the ground (contact shading). A 1024 × 1024 one-channel map over the playable square records what stands where (`js/engine/occlusion.js`); the ground and the grass read it, and things that stand darken toward their own foot. The map is painted in tiles of 64 pixels and only changed tiles are painted again and sent to the card, a few per frame: a felled tree costs one or two small tiles. A building counts only while it is drawn, and the shader reads the map only where the fog is lifted, so the ground never shows what the fog or the model's view hides. One more texture read on the ground. Not in Simple lighting and not on Low.
- Since build 1176 the ground is lived in. Open ground is no longer one flat fill: three slow waves, 40 to 160 units long, make it a little lighter here and darker there (never more than an eighth), lean a meadow toward dry or lush with them, and shade their slopes against the sun as if the land rolled; snow only rolls. And the ground wears: the same map that carries what stands on it carries, in a second channel, where it has been walked. Once a second of match time every drawn unit treads the way it came, a little (a horse more than a man, an engine more than a horse), so some twenty passes show and forty make a path; the earth is packed before each building's door; where a unit fell the ground is churned, and where a building came down it stays bare for some twenty-five minutes. Worn ground goes toward that land's bare soil (earth across a meadow, grey slush across snow, only firmer sand in the desert) and the grass gives out on it tuft by tuft. What is no longer walked grows over again: a path worn bare is green forty minutes after the last foot. Counted in the match's own clock, so a match run faster wears the same paths. Cosmetic state outside every hash: nothing reads it but the shader. Limits: a unit that is not drawn leaves no trace, but wear already on the ground is the match's and stays when the model's view is switched on; the analyzer's still board wears nothing, and a re-simulated replay wears its own ground from where it is played, wiping it on a jump, so it shows the paths of the stretch that was watched and not of the whole match. No further texture read: the wear rides in the contact map. Not in Simple lighting and not on Low.
- Since build 1191 there is air between the eye and the land, and the fog of war is mist (overhaul step A8). What lies beyond the point the camera looks at fades toward the hour's air colour, more the farther it is: about a tenth at the far edge of the ordinary view, a quarter at the far edge of a low shot, nothing at or before the point looked at (`EngineAtmosphere.AIR`, measured in multiples of the look-at distance, so the whole-map view is not a wall of haze). Flames, markers and bars are seen through it; Simple lighting has none. The fog of war was one flat grey sheet at every hour, lighter than the land it covered at night. It is now drawn with two soft fields drifting across each other, which make lighter and denser banks; by night it is dark; and its edge feathers over 8 world units instead of 5 (`js/fog-display.js`). A bank only ever thickens the fog, so nothing it hides shows more. Not built: the sky, sun, moon and stars of the plan. The camera looks down by ten degrees or more with a field of view of twenty, so the horizon never enters the picture (asp67, 22 November: haze only).
- Since build 1193 the light travels through the day, so the shadows do (`renderer.movingSun`; built switched off in build 1192, switched on by asp67 after seeing it; `game.renderer.movingSun = false` in the browser console puts the light back where it always stood). The sun was never in the picture and never moved, so every shadow lay the same way at every hour. With the switch on it swings 52 degrees to either side of where it has always stood, which it passes at noon, and sinks to 27 degrees above the horizon at the day's two ends; the moon travels the same way back by night, and at dusk and dawn the light stands still while one hands over to the other, so it never jumps (`EngineAtmosphere.SUN`, `sunPhase`, `sunDirection`). Mornings light the fronts the camera sees and throw long shadows to the right; evenings put those fronts in shade. Two things to know before switching it on. Open ground is a little darker toward the day's ends (the key light is raised by only part of what the lower angle takes away). And shadow edges shimmer while the light moves: in a still scene at 1280x720 about 5,500 pixels change by more than 4 of 255 from one frame to the next, against none with the light at rest, because the shadow map's grid turns with the light. Whether that is visible in motion has to be judged by eye in a running match, not from stills.
- Since build 1195 a site that is used up gets smaller (overhaul step A5, its first slice). A rock, an ore heap or a berry bush is drawn at full size down to three quarters of what it started with, then at 86 %, 72 % and 58 % of its size as it passes three quarters, a half and a quarter (`EngineRenderer.RES_SHRINK`), about the point it stands on, and its shadow with it. In steps, so a node is rewritten three times in its life and not with every load carried off. A tree stays whole until it is felled. The rest of A5 (building and unit detail by civilization, tree species, rocks and cliffs along the coast) is not built.
- Since build 1196 each land has three kinds of tree (overhaul step A5, its second slice; `EngineRenderer.TREES`). Summer: the oak that was there, a low broad olive with three small crowns, and a tall dark cypress. Winter: the pine, a tall pine (a fifth taller and slimmer, five skirts, straight; since build 1197, in place of a thin fir whose shifted skirts made it look crooked), and the bare tree, which is a fifth of a wood as before. Desert: the acacia, a date palm with eight fronds that rise and hang, and a low flat thorn. Trees of a kind stand together: a slow field over the map picks the kind (`standField`, `_treeSpecies`), so a wood has stands and groves, and one tree in seven strays into its neighbours'. Some trees have a shrub or two at their foot (45 % in summer, 25 % in winter with snow on them, 30 % in the desert), which go when the tree is felled. Which kind a node is drawn as follows from where it stands and its number in the list, so the same map draws the same trees and no rule knows about it. Cost, benchmark of build 1195 against 1196, two runs each in turn: triangles down 5 % (the new kinds are lighter than the acacia and the fine oak crown), draw calls up by 12 to 20 a frame (Low 412 to 426, Balanced 529 to 541, High 953 to 973), frame time inside the noise. The draw calls are the cypress's and the palm's own foliage colour, one more batch per chunk of forest; the olive, the tall pine, the thorn and the undergrowth share a colour their land already had, which is why it is not 50.
- Since build 1198 rocks lie along the shore and headlands of crags stand at the waterline (overhaul step A5, its third slice). The shore was one smooth band of sand all the way round the island. Now there is a rock on the wet sand or in the shallows at about every second place of nine units, one in four of them large, and where a slow field along the shore runs high (about a sixth of it) three or four crags stand shoulder to shoulder, two to three and a half units tall, overlapping into a low wall of rock with a fallen rock or two before it (`_coastRocks`, `EngineRenderer.COAST`). A rock is a coarse ball pushed in and out corner by corner with its underside cut off, every face lit as one plane (`EngineMesh.crag`, 42 faces). They are scenery: every one stands beyond the line a unit can walk to and beyond the map's square, so nothing meets one and nothing could be built there. Laid by the map's seed; drawn at every camera height, in chunks of 256 units, one draw and one shadow shape a chunk, in the land's own stone; hidden while the map's edge beside them is unexplored. Cost, benchmark of build 1196 against 1198: 3 to 15 draw calls and 9 to 27 thousand triangles more a frame, frame time inside the noise. They are low crags, not cliffs with a height the land steps up to: the land is one flat plane, and a real cliff would need ground that rises.
- World-coordinate water normals animate reflected sky and sunlight. The former crossing sine waves produced a repeating bright lattice; they have been replaced by two scales of advected gradient noise, softer sunlight reflections, distance-attenuated fine slopes, and much quieter baked water grain. A separate mask follows the same coast sampler as the terrain and foam, joining the coastal and offshore water treatment. The playable square remains land.
- Greek Bronze/Iron buildings gain limestone courses, column capitals, cornices, terracotta roofs and window details. Decorative parts do not enlarge the measured structural footprint. Other civilizations retain their existing compositions in this milestone.
- Units now use miniature-soldier proportions inspired by the supplied thumbnail: exposed faces, sculpted helmets, rounded armor, convex shields, flared capes and distinct weapons. Workers, ranged units, priests and cavalry riders share the revised faces and cultural headgear; infantry armor grows with tier. Civilian Yamato brims and Persian caps now sit just above the eyebrows instead of sharing the military helmet offset. The four civilization identities and per-seat badges are retained. This is procedural artwork, not an imported or exact recreation of the thumbnail.
- Handheld axes, clubs, swords, bows, crossbows, priest staffs and mounted/chariot spears tilt as complete assemblies around their palm grips and follow the corresponding arm animation. Horses have straight symmetric rest legs, inset leg roots with caps buried inside the body throughout the stride, connected hip pivots, a larger skull and muzzle, fitted bridles and quieter neck motion. Mounted riders have two articulated arms of matching thickness, a weapon grip at the palm, and bent thighs, knees, calves and forward-facing boots around the saddle. Their legs remain seated while the horse trots. Chariot drivers also have two joined arms.
- Every human unit has a visible mouth and civilian-style facial hair: medium full Greek beards in black, brown or blond; fuller black Persian beards; Yamato moustache/chin beards in dark brown or white; short natural Egyptian chin beards. Color variants derive from the unit handle, remain stable through recomposition/replay and do not use simulation randomness.
- Unit parts are baked and grouped by material, bone, team mask and badge accent; cached GPU geometry is shared across seats within each appearance variant while team colors remain per instance. A Greek champion uses 21–22 batches for 52 visible/shadow parts (circle badge), depending on hair color. More triangles still cost GPU time; hardware performance has not been measured.
- Summer/desert trees use one continuous, asymmetrical, scalloped crown instead of overlapping spheres. Angled forks meet the trunk and terminate inside the canopy. Broad foliage color replaces the speckled highlight texture. Winter retains its conifer silhouette. The summer terrain uses a quieter olive palette. Resource locations, quantities, ownership and collision rules are unaffected.

## Compare and tune

Open the chevron beside the minimap:

| Setting | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Auto | Default since build 1150. Measures this computer while it draws and keeps the picture at about 45 frames a second or better: one small step of resolution first, then a lighter tier. Remembers what it found; picking Auto again measures afresh. |
| Low | Atmospheric lighting and water; no cast-shadow map, no contact shading, no worn ground, plain ground without the broad variation. One drawn pixel per page pixel. |
| Balanced | 1024 × 1024 shadow map. Up to 1.5 device pixels per page pixel on a high-density display. |
| High | 2048 × 2048 shadow map, grass, lanterns. Up to 2 device pixels per page pixel. Higher GPU cost. |
| Atmospheric lighting | New light, shadows and water treatment. |
| Simple lighting | Diffuse-light comparison using the same geometry, textures and camera. This is not a complete reproduction of the previous version. |

Graphics quality is saved locally, separately from match/model exports, and a picked tier stays exactly as picked across reloads; only Auto adapts. What a frame costs on a given machine is measured with `?perf=1` and `?perf=bench` ([PERFORMANCE.md](PERFORMANCE.md)). Shadow targets are released on quality changes, and allocation failure falls back to unshadowed rendering. Cast shadows fade out between camera half-heights 100 and 160; overview shots use the existing contact shading instead of stretching a low-resolution shadow across the whole map.

## Verification and limits

- `node --test tests/*.test.cjs` exercises camera/replay state, water-mask alignment, Greek geometry winding, shadow fallback and target disposal, visibility-limited shadow submission, all unit/civilization tiers, baked geometry under limb poses, per-seat tint isolation, stable hair variants, animated weapon grips, connected rider arms and seated legs, symmetric horse stance and connected tree forks.
- Transcript regressions cover the eight-card limit, idle no-op, full ring navigation, preserved reading position, late results, decision-log jumps, bounded state expansion, seat switching and ring eviction. A synthetic 300-turn test with long replies reduced initial mounted text from about 10.8 million to 287,000 characters; offline JavaScript/DOM construction measured roughly 136 ms before and 10 ms after. This excludes browser layout, painting and GPU time and is not an FPS claim. An offline full-application startup also checks the mounted card limit, literal reply escaping, paging and late results, and continues renderer frames with mocked GPU calls after opening the panel.
- Shadow regressions check light-space texel alignment during pans, surface-plane depth comparisons at both map resolutions, and dither state restoration. The approach follows [Microsoft’s shadow-map guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/dxtecharts/common-techniques-to-improve-shadow-depth-maps) on per-texel depth comparisons and texel-aligned projections. GPU image quality still requires an in-browser check.
- World-surface regressions check opacity, repeatability, exact offshore grain phase and gold/stone distinction across all three themes. Navigation regressions cover civilization switches, Back, Main menu, preserved URL options and ordinary campaign/arena return destinations. Real Canvas2D texture sheets were inspected offline; these are texture checks, not GPU scene screenshots.
- Both main and shadow shader pairs compile and link with glslangValidator, including the lower-precision fragment variant.
- An offline application startup check, with real procedural texture generation and mocked GPU calls, reaches all four civilization showcases, checks that every unit/building belongs to the chosen civilization, verifies the five unit classes, worker close-up and civilization-switch URL, and checks moving the camera toolbar between game and analyzer docks.
- Offline mesh inspection checks unit proportions, weapon angles, facial hair, horse profiles and the tree silhouette using flat material swatches and CPU lighting. It does not render the game shaders or establish final image quality.
- Browser visual verification and hardware frame-rate measurements remain pending: the available browser blocks the local preview. The checks above do not establish screenshot quality or browser GPU correctness.

The broader design programme still includes a rendered start-page backdrop, further civilization architecture and unit refinements, richer combat animation, and a more substantial results redesign. Those are not represented as complete by this milestone.
